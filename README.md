# Skate Ski

A cross-country skiing game prototype that runs in the browser, built with three.js. You don't hold a key to accelerate. You ski the way you would on snow: shifting your weight from ski to ski, and timing your kicks and pole pushes.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It loads three.js from cdnjs, so it needs an internet connection.

## Controls

The keyboard, controller and touch screen share one layout. On a controller the sticks stroke and the triggers kick, and you can mix them freely.

| Action | Keyboard | Controller |
|---|---|---|
| Kick (left / right) | ← / → or A / D | LT / RT |
| Snow plow (hold) | ↓ or S | Both sticks down |
| Double pole | Space | LB or RB |
| Switch skating / classic | C | A button |
| Music on / off | M | — |
| Tuck (hold, classic) | ↑ or W | Left stick up / both sticks up |
| Steer (classic and tuck) | Q / E | Left stick left / right |

- **Skating kicks:** hold a skating kick to push it all the way out and ride the glide. Each kick turns you a step away from the kicking leg.
  - Same-leg kicks build into a kick turn, measured from your current heading: the 1st and 2nd turn 5°, the 3rd 15°, and from there each grows by a fixed step so eight quick kicks on one side make a full turn: 180° if you started the run slow or stopped (5°, 5°, 15°, 20.3°, 25.7°, 31°, 36.3°, 41.7°), 90° at skating speed for a tight turn (where every kick after the 2nd is 15°), blending between 1 and 14 km/h. Controller triggers, being slower to pull, get there in six quick kicks (5°, 5°, then even steps: 20°, 35°, 50°, 65° from a standstill). The build only counts kicks that come within the kick-turn window (450 ms) of the last one; slower same-foot kicks restart the build, but from the third same-side kick in a row a turn is never less than 15° (5°, 5°, pause, 15°, pause, 15°…), so a pause doesn't drop you back to 5°. That floor is a slider too. Once a run of same-side kicks has made its full turn (180° from a standstill, 90° at speed), the build starts over: the next kick turns 15° and they grow again from there, so kicking on round doesn't spin faster and faster. A loaded stroke (stick or touch swipe) is timed from when you start pulling back, so its longer wind-up doesn't break the build: loaded strokes turn exactly like plain kicks. The 2nd and 3rd turns, the window, the number of kicks, both full-turn sizes and the speeds are sliders.
  - An opposite-leg kick is measured from the camera heading, so alternating kicks swing evenly around a steady line.
- **Classic kicks:** these must alternate legs. After a kick from a trigger, key or tap, your weight eases back over both skis within about half a second (with the sticks, the stance holds as long as you hold it). On a phone, a swipe up a pad in classic is a stride kick with the same loaded boost as a skate swipe. Letting go of a snow plow returns you to the mode you started it from.
- **Twin-stick strokes:** each stick is one foot.
  - Flick a stick up and out from rest and it's exactly a trigger press for the other leg. Pull it down and in first (load), then throw it up and out, and it's the same kick with a bigger push (Loaded stroke boost, 1.4× by default), skated at a slightly narrower angle so the extra push goes forward rather than side to side. The stroke's angle and length don't change anything.
  - In classic, swap one stick up and one down to kick.
- **Vibration:** each kick buzzes the controller on that side, where the browser supports it (Chrome and Edge, with trigger rumble on Xbox controllers). The level depends on the snow: 5% on groomed trails and lawns, 25% on ungroomed snow, blended across a trail's edge. Around that level, loaded strokes buzz 30% harder, pushes at speed up to 30% lighter, kick turns a little firmer, and no two are quite the same. When a stick is pulled back to load a stroke, that side gives one short buzz at 1%. All three levels are sliders in the Controller group.
- **Animation:** trigger and key kicks animate as V1 (offset), and stick strokes as V2.
- **Touch screens:** the counter shows at the top, and a map button in the top-right corner opens a full-screen map of all four zones. Tap the labelled **Left kick** / **Right kick** pads at the bottom of the screen for a kick (hold the pad to ride the glide).
  - **Swipe up (loaded stroke):** swipe your thumb up a pad. That's the same kick with the loaded boost, animated as V2, exactly like a loaded stick throw; a short flick up counts too. A tap waits up to 90 ms to see whether it's the start of a swipe (lifting or sliding sideways kicks at once). The wait and the swipe distance are sliders in the Touch group.
  - **Snow plow:** press and hold anywhere on the scene above the pads; let go to stop.
  - Between the pads sits the only other control, a toggle: **\/** means skate and **||** means classic. A speaker button in the top-left corner mutes the music on any device. Double-tap and pinch zoom, text selection and the long-press Copy/Share menu are all disabled so fast kicking doesn't disturb the page on iOS.

## Levels

The map is four times its old area and split into zones, each locked until you clear the one before it.

- **Terminals:** each of the first three zones has outpost terminals beside its trails: 2 in zone 1, 3 in zone 2 and 3 in zone 3. Ski up to one and start it, and its orbs light up on a loop of trail that leaves the terminal and comes back to it (10 per terminal, 15 in zone 3; each terminal has its own loop, a few hundred metres long). Collect them all and ski back: the terminal powers on by itself as you reach it, with no stop or prompt. Power every terminal in a zone, then ski up to a control to open what it guards (see below). Zone 1's terminals run both the east gate and the south gate, and zone 2's run both drawbridges; zone 3's power the mine's generators and lights and let the transport rooms' portals open. Only one terminal runs at a time. Each terminal has an antenna light: red while dormant, yellow (pulsing) while running and green once powered. While one terminal is running, the others that are still dormant go dark (they can't be started yet); powered ones stay green. Its screen and a row of lights (one per orb), which fill as you collect its orbs, match.
- **Gate and bridge controls:** the east gate, the south gate and the two drawbridges each have their own control, and each opens on its own when you reach it: and every one can be opened from either side: each gate's lintel has lights on both faces, and each drawbridge has a post on each bank. It has one indicator light per terminal in its zone, red until that terminal is powered and then green, and an antenna light that stays red until you ski up to the control with every terminal powered. That opens the way, and the antenna turns green. Ski up to it before then and it tells you how many terminals are still to power.
- **The altar:** on the dais at the centre of the Broken Circle. Stop at it any time to wake it (you don't have to power the zone 3 terminals first): the 20 purple crystals appear. Bring them all in, then come back to the altar to open the Crystal Gate.
- **Prompts:** skiing into range of a terminal or the altar when there's something to do there (start it, wake it, open the gate) interrupts the game with a full-screen prompt: you don't need to slow down, the skier holds still (keeping their speed) and it takes all input until you answer it. Keyboard: Space (or Enter) to go ahead, X (or Esc) to cancel. Controller: A to go ahead, X (or B) to cancel; the triggers don't cancel, since it's easy to squeeze one skiing up. Touch: tap the buttons. Anything that's only news (another terminal is running, orbs still out) is a short message instead of a prompt. Either way it won't come again until you've moved away and come back.
- **Orb magnet:** an orb within 7 m (a slider in the Controller group) is drawn in to you, faster as it closes, so you only need to pass near it.
- **Counter and map:** the counter at the top shows the running terminal's orbs, or the crystals once the altar is awake, or otherwise how many of the zone's terminals are powered. The map shows the terminals (red dormant, yellow running, green powered), the running terminal's orbs, the crystals and the altar.
- **Energy bridges (post-game, first version):** when the Crystal Gate opens, a network of elevated energy bridges lights up. Its on-ramps sit away from the landmarks, each marked by a portal: two plain white poles that, once the network is lit, gain caps of light and an arch between them and shown on the map as rings, with the network as dashed purple lines:
  - **Zone 1:** curls up round the inside foot of the mountain ring from the north, following its curve as it climbs, then crosses the ring's south-west ridge.
  - **The Spine:** runs down the west edge, over the river and the divide (about 50 m up), then makes a long descent east along the divide's south flank to a portal in zone 4.
  - **The Lake Ring:** a connector branches off the Spine to a ring around the frozen lake. From the ring, the East Arc crosses the river and comes down along the east edge's mountains into zone 2, and a ramp drops into zone 3.
  - **Only while you're on it:** a bridge segment only appears while you're on it, plus the segments branching off when you near its end, and everything fades once you ski off, so nothing hangs over the map.
  - **Riding:** ski onto a ramp through its portal and you're on. You ski as usual on a deck that glides like groomed trail, and a climb costs you no speed. Glowing railings with light pulses keep you on. At a junction you take whichever deck you steer onto. You get off at the foot of a ramp.
  - **Pylons and junctions:** pylons are few and far between, only where the deck is high over open ground. They're real structures: always standing in the landscape (and solid), whether or not you're on the bridge; only the deck of light comes and goes. Each stands only where it's out of the way: well off the trails and lawns, out of the buildings and camps, clear of the river and lake, the terminals and orbs and the herds, and never in zone 5. A tree or boulder right at a pylon's foot is cleared. Decks run level for the last stretch into a junction. Trees and ice boulders in a bridge's path have been cleared. Where a branch leaves the Lake Ring it rides alongside the ring before peeling away, so every fork is a clean Y.
  - **On-ramps:** each ground portal (zones 1 to 4) stands on a level pad of snow, with its run-up kept clear of trees and boulders. You only get on a ramp through its portal, heading into it (within about 55°): not backwards, and not in over the side. Ski into a portal the wrong way (down the ramp's line toward its foot) and you're carried out the far side on a low teardrop loop that swings round beside the ramp, on whichever side is clearer, and joins its line about 54 m up, facing uphill. The loops climb no steeper than 20% and aren't shown on the map. Getting off at a ramp's foot, that portal leaves you alone until you've skied 10 m clear of it, so you can always leave. Each loop is really a whole bridge of its own, the loop and then an exact copy of the ramp from where they meet up to the ramp's junction, so there's no seam or hand-over: only the bridge you're on shows. Coming down from the junction you always take the straight ramp.
  - **Trail assist on the bridges:** trail assist follows the deck you're on, the same way it follows a trail, and at a junction it takes the branch you're pointed along.
  - **While you're up there:** trails, orbs, terminals and the herds below are out of reach. Your skis leave glowing purple marks instead of snow tracks, and the moment you lay the next one, every mark before it starts fading away to nothing.
  - **Testing:** the **Energy bridges on (testing)** slider in dev view's Testing group lights them before the gate opens.
- **Zone 5 (hidden):** once the giant is standing, there's one more portal underneath it, between its legs. That's the only way into zone 5, and the bridge from it isn't joined to the rest of the network or shown on the map. It's a long, sweeping climb: east over zone 4 and round above the far herd, up to the mountain tops, then due south over a rounded crest (the mountainside carries straight on from zone 4's slope, with no seam) and down onto a high plateau beyond the map's edge, level with the mountain tops, that ends in a sheer cliff. Everything there is in one straight line: you land facing the ring of diamonds, the elders, the Starway's portal, the observatory and, out past the cliff, the planet. The fog lifts and the sky opens onto space: a field of stars, the ringed violet planet and two moons. The observatory is a stepped platform, a ring of columns capped with glowing crystal and a domed hall (no telescope). Purple crystal grows everywhere except on the line and under the bridges, the whole plateau (not the mountains round it) is groomed, and natives in bright robes play drums, long horns, harps and crystal chimes around the platform and out at the cliff edge. The plateau is a promontory: past its side walls and beyond the cliff, the ground falls away out of sight.
  - **The ring and the elders:** a ring of six diamond insignias lies on dead-level snow, set so the way through, north to south, stays open. Only three of the diamonds show at first. Across the way beyond the ring stand the three elders, small grown moose in bright drapes with gold hems and gold crowns, shoulder to shoulder; the middle one, in royal purple, leads. Bring your calf onto the plateau and it leaves you for an open diamond, which lights violet under it. Two attendants in white and gold robes, carrying gold staffs, hurry over and stand either side of it with their arms raised, and a drape and crown of its own appear on it. It stays there for good (it's saved, and it never goes back to its herd). When the third calf is crowned, the other three diamonds appear and the elders walk round the calves into the ring, onto them; when all three are in place the Starway lights. You can't send a calf home until the ring is complete. Any more calves you bring are crowned too, without a word in the game: they watch from two arcs of six well south of the ring, either side of the way through and clear of the Starway's portal, alternating sides as they come and filling from the observatory end.
  - **Secret ending (spoiler):** nothing in the game mentions it. When all 15 calves are crowned, everyone not in the ring (the twelve watching calves, the musicians and the attendants) strolls over (no rush: the procession builds to its walking pace over about 15 s) to form two conga lines, staged to be watched from the ring: each winds back and forth between the ring and the cliff beside the observatory, on its own side of the way through, never within 10 m of the centre line. Fireworks burst over the plateau whether or not you're on a bridge, and six vast translucent moose appear out in space, each bigger than the planet and slowly shifting colour: three either side of it, standing still and spread in a fan about 24° apart as seen from the ring, all facing the ring.
  - **The Starway:** the one light bridge to the planet. Its white-pole portal stands just past where the elders stood, and it stays dark until the ring is complete (ski onto it before then and you're told how far along you are). Your calf can't come along, since it will already have joined the court. The ramp climbs evenly over the observatory's stepped platform, through the gateway arch and straight through the domed hall (it has a wide doorway north and south, and the dome fades while you're inside), and out over the cliff. From there it runs straight to the planet and into it. Once you're inside, the planet's shell is drawn from the inside only, so it never hides you from the camera as it follows you in. The bridge then spirals down a funnel of streaming violet and white stripes, shaped to sit just under the bridge (it meets the bridge's outer edge and never crosses it), and, after a last tightening curl, plunges straight into the black hole at its throat: the bridge never visibly ends. Over the last stretch the black hole swells, swallowing the end of the bridge and then you, until the whole screen is black; only then does it ask what you want to do, so you never see the bridge just end. Out in the open, before you enter the planet, fireworks burst in the sky ahead and to either side; inside the planet they stop.
  - **The black hole:** the bridge slows you to a stop at its edge and asks: **Return to the observatory** puts you back at the foot of the hidden way, looking down the line. **Reset all progress** asks you to confirm (Space or A to erase, X or B to go back). Erasing resets every terminal, orb, crystal, gate and bridge, sends every crowned calf home to its herd, and starts you again from the beginning, but it keeps count of how many times you've finished. After your first, your jacket and hat are royal purple, and you wear the court's own crown (the same gold band, beaded spikes and crystal diamond the elders and calves wear) over your hat, a flat gold belt, and the court's gold diamond on your back, like the crowned calves' garb.
- **Heading arrow:** the arrow on the snow in front of you is off by default; **Heading arrow** in dev view's Testing group turns it on (kept in this browser).
- **Plateau camera:** on zone 5's plateau (on the ground) and all along the Starway, the camera eases down low, lifts its gaze to the horizon and widens a little, so the sky, the planet and whatever stands out in space are framed over the court. Inside the planet it rises instead into a slight bird's-eye view, tilted down the funnel so you can see the turns below and the black hole at the bottom; nothing from outside (fireworks, anything in space) shows in there.
- **Checkpoint:** the terminal or altar you last used (starting or powering a terminal, waking the altar or opening the Crystal Gate) is your checkpoint: a new visit starts you on the trail beside it.
- **Saving:** terminals, orbs, the altar and crystals are saved in your browser, so gates and bridges stay open between visits (saves from before the terminals keep any zone you'd finished). Your calf, the giant and the crowned calves are saved too. **Reset skier** in dev view clears that progress, and **Checkpoint: altar** beside it makes the altar your checkpoint and puts you there (setting the **Crystals collected** slider does the same). **Checkpoint: zone 5** puts your checkpoint at the foot of the hidden way on the plateau (and stands the giant, so the way back is open). The **Calves crowned** slider puts that many calves in the ring straight away (or sends them home). For testing, the **Zone 1 / 2 / 3 terminals powered** and **Crystals collected** sliders in dev view's Tuning panel set them directly; maxing zone 1 opens both gates, maxing zone 2 lowers both drawbridges, and maxing the crystals opens the Crystal Gate.


- **Zone 1 (north-west):** the original trail network, enclosed by an irregular range of impassable mountains. Power its two terminals to open the **East Gate** in the wide, flat pass on the east side (they open the South Gate too).
- **Zone 2 (north-east):** past the gate. It's hillier, with five winding trails (green to black) that all lead to the river. Power its three terminals to lower its two **drawbridges** into zone 3, each from its own control. Steel-plated ramps on slate abutments meet the snow flush on both banks, and the lowered deck skis like groomed trail. A spur ridge runs from zone 1's mountains to the river, with a pass cut through it: the green **Spur Pass** leaves the Hollow Loop and runs west through it to the lower drawbridge at the foot of the Switchbacks, so the strip below zone 1 is part of zone 2 now, reached without crossing the river.

Zone 2 has a processing plant, a mine excavator, a greenhouse complex, a bio lab and a comms array. Smaller equipment (weather stations, fuel tanks, solar arrays, haul vehicles) sits just off the trails. Buildings are solid and show on the map. What you bump into is read off each model's actual shape at body height, so walls are where you see them, and you can ski under overhead parts such as the Spine Arch.
- **Gates and bridges:** the East and South Gates and both river bridges are outpost tech, like the buildings. The gates have white pylons with light strips, a barrier of panels out to the slopes, a steel lintel and armoured door leaves; the lintel carries the gate's control (indicator lights and an antenna, red until opened, then green). The bridges are both drawbridges: steel decks with light rails, white bollards and white lifting pylons. The Crystal Gate keeps its own look.
- **Zone 3 (the southern half):** across the river, around a frozen lake you can ski across (the ice runs like groomed trail). Six trails circle and cross the lake, with three terminals. Instead of outpost buildings, it holds the remnants of an alien civilisation: the Sleeping Ribs (the bones of a full-grown purple moose about eleven times the size of the living ones, lying where it fell asleep: the hump of spines over its shoulders, an arched rib cage over a cluster of purple crystal where its heart was, six folded legs, and its skull and palmate antlers on the snow with the tentacle beard scattered as beads of bone), the Spine Arch (which Bridge Road passes under), the Watcher spire and the Broken Circle, plus bone shards. A curving groomed tongue joins it to the nearest trail; the tongue is about as wide as the lawn, swells and narrows along its length, and tapers toward the trail. Lawns and tongues that come close melt together into one shape. Trails and lawns are drawn as one groomed shape with no rim line: groomed snow meets the open snow along a soft edge, and every corner (bends, junctions and trail-to-lawn joins) is rounded off. They ski like trail, stop at water and rock, and aren't signed or shown on the map.
- **Purple crystals:** once the altar is woken, 20 crystals appear: 5 in zone 1, 5 in zone 2 and 10 in zone 3. Each sits beside an off-trail landmark (a building, ruin or camp), always in the open with nothing built over it, marked by a 10 m column of purple light you can see from far off. The counter switches to crystals, and the map shows them as purple diamonds.
- **Zone 4:** south of zone 3 beyond a mountain divide across the whole map, the same size as zone 3. Bringing all 20 crystals back to the altar swings open the 21 m **Crystal Gate** in the divide, south of the Broken Circle. The gate stands in a narrow notch between sheer rock walls, with tall ribbed walls from its pylons into the rock, so nothing of zone 4 shows until it opens. Zone 4 is open snowfields with no buildings or people: winding Frost Road (blue), scattered outcrops of purple crystal, herds of purple moose, and a giant. The giant is a grown moose as big as the skeleton by the lake, asleep in the middle of Frost Road's loop on a broad groomed lawn, which the green **Giant's Way** crosses as it cuts across the loop, its chest rising and falling and a cloud of breath rolling from its muzzle every few seconds; it's solid. Bring your calf (below) near it while it's in view and it rises to its feet and stays standing. Standing, only its legs are solid, so you can ski right under its belly. Five herds straddle Frost Road, so you ski straight through them, and another lies at the far southern end of the Herd Trail. In each, some graze and wander and some lie asleep in the snow, with antlerless calves and big bulls one and a half to two times the size of a grown moose.
- **Your calf:** the first antlerless calf you come close to in zone 4 leaves its herd and follows you from then on, into every zone, and it's still with you next visit. It runs over from wherever it is to fall in behind you (and runs in again at the start of a visit), then walks in your tracks about 6 m behind, matching your speed and closing the gap if it falls back. It never blocks you, and it stamps six-legged hoof prints in the snow. ## Interface

- **Play view (default):**
  - Map and distance in the bottom left.
  - Grade, cadence and speed in the bottom right.
  - Stick diagrams with LT / RT readouts and the current mode in the centre. Trigger kicks show on the opposite stick, and when you're idle the diagrams demo the intended rhythm.
  - A controller-layout panel in the top right.

  - **Crown room:** at the crown door, wearing the reward outfit.
  - **Space port:** in the north transport room, carrying all 30 crown crystals.
  - **Ceremony 2:** at the observatory, carrying all 30 crown crystals.
  - **Ceremony 3:** at the observatory, with all 15 calves crowned.

  - **Copy position** copies it (with the camera's) to the clipboard.

## Mechanics

- **Skating:** your body rides the ski you're on, so your path weaves around the line between your skis. The skate V widens or narrows in fixed steps with speed and slope, so both sides stay even.
- **Camera:** it follows the line between your skis, so alternating strides cancel out and only real turns move the view.
- **Ski tracks:** marks are straight, the way a ski is, and are reset when you drift off a ski's line. Kick turns leave a herringbone pattern. Older marks fade into the snow as new ones are laid: the newest 50 are at the mark strength (40% by default, a slider), then about 38%, 33% and 25% for the next three groups of 10, and anything older than 80 marks back stays at 15%. Fading mixes each mark's colour toward the snow it lies on (groomed, open snow or lake ice) rather than making it see-through, so crossing marks never darken each other. Skis lie along the slope under them, tilted from tip to tail and side to side, and always sit on top of their marks.
- **Terrain and trails:**
  - Rolling terrain with a ridge and a valley, and gravity acts along the skis.
  - Groomed trails with green, blue and black ratings, and scattered trees: six in zone 1, the East Gate connector, and five in zone 2.
  - The open snow is a light, clear ice blue rather than white (only groomed snow is white), and the steep mountain faces are a paler icy blue. It's textured with wind ripples (sastrugi) that come and go in patches, under broad soft drifts. Their brightest crests are a pale blue, so only groomed snow is white. The two patterns repeat at different sizes, so the ground reads in perspective without a grid's uniform look. There are no contour lines. Trees grow only on the valley floors and the mountains' lower skirts, never on steep ground. Among them, faceted ice boulders (the same ice as round the zone 2 beacon) lie scattered through the open snow: lone boulders, clusters, upright shards and flat slabs in three tints of ice, kept off trails and lawns. They're scenery only: you ski straight through them.
- **Palette:** everything shares one wintry palette, in shades and tints of a few core colours: slate blue and snow white for the outpost and the people's gear; blue-green spruce; grey-brown bark, timber and robes; muted rust and ochre accents (the skier's jacket, tarps and some parkas); glacier blue for the natives; and dusk violet for the moose and the crystals.
  - Mountains, the river and the map edge stop you. The ground loads in tiles as you ski, and a coarse mesh carries the distant skyline.
  - Ungroomed snow is slower, except in classic. Double poles keep their full push off the trail.
- **Trail assist:** gently steers your stride toward the trail ahead. It aims across the centre line at the mirror of your offset (the **centring** slider sets how far), so drifting to one edge pulls you back through the middle instead of letting you settle off to the side. Centring fades out near junctions and trail ends, and after switching trails the assist (and its centring) eases back in over the trail-switch time, so changing trails never yanks you sideways. It lets go when you lean, kick-turn or steer away. At junctions it works with kick turns instead:
  - **Snap to trails:** within 20 m of a junction, a same-side kick that would swing past one of the trails leaving it (or end just short of one) stops lined up with it instead. That includes the trail you came in on, so a full kick turn stops pointing back the way you came. A snapped kick doesn't use up a step of the build: the next kick carries the step the snapped one would have had (for example 15°, 25°, snap onto the trail, then 40°), so you can carry on round to the next trail.
  - **Settle:** after a kick turn at a junction, the assist comes back as soon as the kick-turn window closes and eases in quickly (0.3 s instead of 1.5 s), pulling you onto the trail you're nearly lined up with.
  - The snap distance is a slider in the Trail assist group (0 turns it off).
- **Skier:** V2 for stick strokes (load tall, push and compress, recover) and V1 offset poling for trigger and key kicks, with tuck, snow plow and classic poses.
- **Tuning:** every setting is a slider in the Tuning panel, grouped into Controller, Touch, Kicks and strides, Skate angle, Snow and glide, Classic/poling/braking, Trail assist, Camera and Testing. Only sliders you've moved are remembered in your browser; the rest follow the current defaults. Physics runs in fixed steps of at most 1/60 s, so it behaves the same at any frame rate. The stick debug recorder logs your stick input so you can tune the input thresholds.

## The underground and the space station

The underground was built in a sandbox copy of the game and is now merged into it, west of the map. Its 3D models and lighting are still placeholders to be refined.

- **The miners' things:** across the ungroomed floor of every room (thickest round the transport rooms) lie the mine's leavings, all solid except the lamps. The mine has been worked out: there are no crystals anywhere underground except in the crown room (its pillar, the clusters round it and on its walls, and the 30 on the spiral) and on the crown door's columns. They never sit on a course, under a bridge, in the street's building rows or in the crown room:
  - drill rigs, empty ore carts, and empty conveyor sections;
  - crate stacks, barrels and cable reels;
  - prefab huts and compressors;
  - lamp posts and striped barriers.
- **Lights:** the cave has a couple of dozen light spots, but only a fixed pool of six real lights. The crown room keeps just two gentle ones (its pillar, lanterns and crystals glow on their own). Each frame they take the places of the nearest spots, which keeps every graphics card responsive (the crown room used to freeze some).
- **Landmark camera:** coming up to the crystal gate, the crown door or the cargo bin at the space port, the camera eases into zone 5's low, upward-looking framing.

- **The mine:** a rocky ridge on the empty west side of zone 3, reached by the green **Mine Road** from the Watcher Climb. A cut leads into its east face, to a timber portal. The cut's floor has exactly the tunnel's cross-section where they meet, so you ski straight through the portal and underground, with no loading or teleport.
- **The mine shaft:** a horseshoe tunnel (about 8 m wide, 5.6 m high, steel arches and lamps). It winds down under the ridge (up to about 18%), then runs west under the map's west mountains and straight through the gap in the village loop to the hub, about 540 m in all. It never touches the ring.
- **Layout (west of the map):** the hub is the high point, with five spoke rooms round it. The village loop, the low point, wanders round everything except the east, where the shaft comes in. Every room except the crown room has a hall to the hub and another to the ring, and the halls run smoothly between the heights of the rooms at their two ends.
- **The three skills parks:** each drops about 28 m from its hub end to its ring end. They use the normal snow physics (the earlier icy, low-friction snow is gone), so only the slope sets your speed.
  - **Roller Coaster:** big rollers down the descent.
  - **Berm Run:** a slalom line on a level trail, with a raised berm beside it on the outside of every turn (rising from about 2 m off the line, highest at the apex) to carry you round.
  - **Half-pipe Chute:** a deep trough down the descent to weave up and down.
  - There are no jumps or drops anywhere.
- **Lift & Slalom:** a magic carpet up one side, and a slalom down the other with red and blue gates on a weaving line, a turn every 18 m.
- **Magic carpets:** every skills park has one up its side, from the village loop to the hub (about 200 m and a 36 m climb), in its own tall tunnels at each end. Each is a light bridge like the others: a portal at each end, getting on only through a portal heading in, a deck of light that shows only while you ride it, railings of light, and a climb that costs no speed. The carpets are always open.
  - **The grade:** it's even, about 22–27%, with gentle changes. It starts and finishes level at the portals and never dips. It's the lowest steady climb that clears the floor everywhere, so where the floor drops away it floats on pylons.
  - **Tested:** all four ride end to end in both directions.
- **The crown room:** a tall round room (46 m across, 72 m to the roof) that opens only onto the village loop. Its **crown door** stands right on the street's inner edge, in a stone facade flush with the dwellings on either side and running from the floor up into the roof. The door is a floor-to-ceiling slab of dark violet stone, 13 m wide and 24 m tall, with gold edging and a great gold crown on it. In front of the facade are observatory-style columns capped with glowing crystal: a tall pair flanking the door under a great arch with a crystal keystone, and smaller arches over dark recesses to either side. Lights wash the facade. Behind the door is a grand hall about 14 m wide and 27 m high.
  - **Opening:** the door is sealed (you're told so if you come close) unless you're wearing the purple reward outfit. Ski up to it in the outfit and it sinks into the floor. It seals again once you've taken the outfit off and gone.
  - **Inside:** at the centre stands an enormous glowing crystal pillar rising from the floor into the roof, with crystals clustered round its foot. Round it, a stone path (the **Crystal Spiral**) winds up through 2¾ turns, widening as it climbs like the black hole's funnel in reverse. It's walled both sides and lit by lanterns, and climbs about 40 m at roughly 9%. You get on at its foot (a short straight lead-in heading north) and it carries on up. The top is a closed lookout high under the roof, so you come back down the way you went up.
  - **The 30 crown crystals:** they're spaced evenly up the spiral, and you pick one up by skiing through it. While you're in the room, the HUD shows **n / 30 Crown crystals**. They're saved in your browser.
- **The village loop (cave city):** a long, organic cavern (like the mountain range round zone 1), about 50 m wide with a 38 m roof. It's lined on both walls with abandoned adobe dwellings in terraced rows, three deep and climbing the walls, with doorways and windows facing the street. Its two ends, either side of the shaft, come into the transport rooms. It has two skiable levels:
  - **The street:** straight and level down the middle, between the first rows of buildings (the Village Loop course).
  - **The terraces:** two light bridges, the North Terrace and the South Terrace (about 550 m each), 7.5 m up. Each runs along the first row of buildings and winds from wall to wall over the street. Each has on and off ramps at every skills park entrance:
    - **End ramps:** one at each end. The Roller Coaster / Half-pipe end rises along the outer side, clear of that park's carpet; the crown-room end runs along the inner side.
    - **Off-ramps:** two in the middle by the Lift & Slalom (south) or Berm Run (north) entrance, forking off the terrace, one for each direction.
    - **Junctions:** at a junction you ride whichever deck you steer onto, and the deck you can carry on to shows as you come up to it. They follow the same rules as the light bridges up top:
    - **Portals:** each ramp's foot has a portal, two white posts that gain caps of light and an arch once the bridges are lit. That happens when the Crystal Gate opens, or with the **Energy bridges on** testing switch.
    - **Getting on and off:** you get on only through a portal, heading in along the ramp (within about 55°), never backwards or over the side. You come off at the far foot, and that portal ignores you until you've skied 10 m clear of it.
    - **The deck:** it shows only while you're riding it, fading in as you get on and out as you leave. White pylons hold it up over open floor, though never in the middle of the street.
    - **Clearance:** any dwelling whose footprint comes under a bridge is cut down to sit beneath it, so nothing pokes through the deck.
    - **Riding:** railings of light hold you and a climb costs no speed. Street skiers ski under the bridges.

    Tested: both bridges ride end to end in both directions, and a side approach doesn't board.
- **Transport rooms and portals:** each has two generators and a steel wall across its east end, with a doorway. Each portal (north and south) opens like a gate:
  - **Controls:** a post stands beside the doorway on each side, one in the transport room and one on the station by the portal arm. Each has five lights, one per zone 3 terminal (red, then green once powered), and an antenna light that turns green once the portal is open.
  - **Label:** "NORTH PORTAL · 3 / 5 TERMINALS", or "OPEN".
  - **Opening:** ski up to either post. If zone 3's terminals aren't all powered, it tells you how many are. Once they are, the portal opens: the dark membrane starts to glow and you ski straight through.
  - **Saving:** open portals are saved.
  - The terminals also bring up the cave's lamps and lights, which are dim until then.
- **The space station:** built from the concept art (models and lighting still to be refined). There's a domed hub with a band of light and a spire, and a 9 m metal walkway ring round it. A raised deck runs round the hub's outside, 8 m up, reached by a spiral ramp that leaves the walkway and arrives on the deck centred on each, level wherever it overlaps them. Eight dock arms each have a walkway on both sides of a docked ship, joined in a loop round the ship's nose so each dock is a loop out and back; the ship waiting for crystals is bigger, at the east dock. Every deck has depth (steel sides and underside), the walkways are trimmed to meet the round walkway cleanly, and the railings break wherever another deck joins. Two portal arms run out to the two portals, each framed by a ring. Far below is the ice planet you ski on, with snowfields, frozen seas and cloud, and a blue rim of atmosphere. The decks are metal plates with light strips, all railed, and glide like groomed trail. The cave and station form a figure eight. The station is a world of its own: from the cave you see it only through the two doorways, and from the station you see only space, the planet and, through the doorways, the cave. So it can sit on the entrance side, overlapping the shaft and the map on the map, without either showing from the other.
- **Underground:** below ground, the floor, walls and trails are the cave's own, and the surface (its buildings, people and trails) doesn't count. The light and fog fade from daylight to the cave's dark violet over the first 25 m of the shaft. The camera comes in close and low in the halls and never goes through a wall or the roof.
- **How it's built:** each room is a chain of overlapping circles and each hall a tunnel along a centre line. Every shape gives its own floor (curving up into its walls) and roof (curving down to meet them); the cave's floor is the lowest of them and its roof the highest, so halls cut cleanly through room walls. A 16 m lookup grid means each point only checks the shapes near it. The floor and roof are built in tiles that stream in round you like the surface's, with the same snow and groomed-trail texturing. Where the shaft meets the ridge's face, the surface ground isn't drawn inside the tunnel's outline, so you see into the mine. Near a portal the view is drawn in two passes: the far world first, masked to the doorways and clipped to its own side of the portals' wall, then this side over it (with the doorways' true depth laid down first, so nothing from the far world shows through this side's floor). Your ski tracks belong to the cave only. Within about 30 m of a powered doorway the camera comes in close behind you, as at the mine's entrance, and it never swings round through the wall, so you look through the doorway rather than over it.
- **Map:** the cave's floor plan and the station's decks show as a faint fill with their courses dashed; underground, the corner map switches to the cave.
- **Testing:** **Cave: the mine**, **Cave: the hub**, **Cave: the station** and **Cave: the crown door** in dev view's Testing group. **Reward outfit (opens the crown door): on/off** is the outfit toggle, and **Reset crown crystals** puts the 30 crystals back. **Checkpoint: village loop** and **Checkpoint: station** set your checkpoint there (kept between visits, so the page starts you there) and put you there. The **Zone 3 terminals powered** slider (max) powers the generators and opens the portals.

### End game

- **The ceremony:** bring 3 calves to the observatory. Once the elders have taken their places in the ring and you're near it, the two attendants in white walk over, stand either side of you with their arms raised, and dress you in the court's purple (the reward outfit). An altar like zone 3's, but larger, then rises from the snow in the middle of the ring and asks for 30 purple crystals to complete the ceremony. The Starway stays dark until then.
- **The crown room:** the purple outfit opens the crown door in the village loop, and the 30 crown crystals are up the spiral inside. You carry them until you spend them.
- **Spending the 30, one of two ways:**
  - **At the altar:** offer them to complete the ceremony. The altar sinks away, the cosmic moose appear out in space (you no longer need every calf for them) and the Starway lights.
  - **On the ship:** the ship waits at the station's big east dock (Dock 1), its engines glowing red, with a crowd of crew along the dock. Its cargo bin stands on a bracket off the main walkway's outer edge at Dock 1, right behind the ship's engines: open, glowing purple inside, the court's gold diamond on its sides, with a column of light above it. Ski the main walkway past the bin and the crew ask for the crystals: the old mine is stripped bare and the new one is still being dug, so they're stranded. Give them the crystals and leave the planet, and everything begins again in your own orange. Or stay on the station. Once you've given the crystals to the court, the crew are still stranded.
- **Starting again:** through the black hole you keep the purple outfit. Leaving on the ship you go back to the standard orange.
- **Hidden ending:** crowning all 15 calves still brings the conga lines (and the cosmic moose, if they aren't out already).
- **Saving:** the outfit, the ceremony's stage and the crown crystals (collected and spent) are saved in your browser. The dev **Reset skier** puts the ceremony and the crystals back but leaves the outfit alone.
- **Testing:** in dev view's Testing group, **Reward outfit** toggles the outfit, and **Ceremony** steps through not yet, altar waiting and complete.
- **Tested:** the whole run, in order:
  - the elders reach the ring, the attendants dress you and the altar rises;
  - with no crystals the altar tells you what it needs;
  - offering the 30 lights the Starway and brings out the cosmic moose;
  - the ship then says your crystals went to the court;
  - the black hole restart keeps the purple;
  - in a fresh run, leaving on the ship with 30 restarts you in orange.
