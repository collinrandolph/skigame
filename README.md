# Skate Ski

A cross-country skiing game prototype that runs in the browser, built with three.js. You don't hold a key to accelerate. You ski the way you would on snow: shifting your weight from ski to ski, and timing your kicks and pole pushes.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It loads three.js from cdnjs, so it needs an internet connection.

## Controls

The keyboard, controller and touch screen share one layout. On a controller the sticks stroke and the triggers kick, and you can mix them freely.

| Action | Keyboard | Controller |
|---|---|---|
| Kick (left / right) | ← / → or A / D | LT / RT |
| Snow plow (hold) | ↓ or S | Both sticks down |
| Double pole | Space | LB or RB |
| Switch skating / classic | C | A button |
| Music on / off | M | — |
| Tuck (hold, classic) | ↑ or W | Left stick up / both sticks up |
| Steer (classic and tuck) | Q / E | Left stick left / right |

- **Skating kicks:** hold a skating kick to push it all the way out and ride the glide. Each kick turns you a step away from the kicking leg.
  - Same-leg kicks build into a kick turn, measured from your current heading: the 1st and 2nd turn 5°, the 3rd 15°, and from there each grows by a fixed step so six quick kicks on one side make a full turn: 180° if you started the run slow or stopped (… 33.3°, 51.7°, 70°), 90° at skating speed (… 18.3°, 21.7°, 25°) for a tight turn, blending between 1 and 14 km/h. Controller triggers, being slower to pull, get there in five quick kicks (5°, 5°, then even steps: 30.8°, 56.7°, 82.5° from a standstill). The build only counts kicks that come within the kick-turn window (450 ms) of the last one; slower same-foot kicks restart the build, but from the third same-side kick in a row a turn is never less than 15° (5°, 5°, pause, 15°, pause, 15°…), so a pause doesn't drop you back to 5°. That floor is a slider too. Once a run of same-side kicks has made its full turn (180° from a standstill, 90° at speed), the build starts over: the next kick turns 15° and they grow again from there, so kicking on round doesn't spin faster and faster. A loaded stroke (stick or touch slingshot) is timed from when you start pulling back, so its longer wind-up doesn't break the build: loaded strokes turn exactly like plain kicks. The 2nd and 3rd turns, the window, the number of kicks, both full-turn sizes and the speeds are sliders.
  - An opposite-leg kick is measured from the camera heading, so alternating kicks swing evenly around a steady line.
- **Classic kicks:** these must alternate legs. Letting go of a snow plow returns you to the mode you started it from.
- **Twin-stick strokes:** each stick is one foot.
  - Flick a stick up and out from rest and it's exactly a trigger press for the other leg. Pull it down and in first (load), then throw it up and out, and it's the same kick with a bigger push (Loaded stroke boost, 1.4× by default), skated at a slightly narrower angle so the extra push goes forward rather than side to side. The stroke's angle and length don't change anything.
  - In classic, swap one stick up and one down to kick.
- **Vibration:** each kick buzzes the controller on that side, where the browser supports it (Chrome and Edge, with trigger rumble on Xbox controllers). The level depends on the snow: 5% on groomed trails and lawns, 25% on ungroomed snow, blended across a trail's edge. Around that level, loaded strokes buzz 30% harder, pushes at speed up to 30% lighter, kick turns a little firmer, and no two are quite the same. When a stick is pulled back to load a stroke, that side gives one short buzz at 1%. All three levels are sliders in the Controller group.
- **Animation:** trigger and key kicks animate as V1 (offset), and stick strokes as V2.
- **Touch screens:** the counter shows at the top, and a map button in the top-right corner opens a full-screen map of all four zones. Tap the labelled **Left kick** / **Right kick** pads at the bottom of the screen for a kick (hold the pad to ride the glide).
  - **Slingshot (loaded stroke):** pull your thumb down the pad, then swipe it back up. That's the same kick with the loaded boost, animated as V2, exactly like a loaded stick throw. Letting go while it's pulled back fires it too. A tap waits up to 90 ms for a pull before it kicks (lifting or sliding sideways kicks at once). The wait, pull and throw distances are sliders in the Touch group.
  - **Snow plow:** press and hold anywhere on the scene above the pads; let go to stop.
  - Between the pads sits the only other control, a toggle: **\/** means skate and **||** means classic. A speaker button in the top-left corner mutes the music on any device. Double-tap and pinch zoom, text selection and the long-press Copy/Share menu are all disabled so fast kicking doesn't disturb the page on iOS.

## Levels

The map is four times its old area and split into zones, each locked until you clear the one before it.

- **Terminals:** each of the first three zones has outpost terminals beside its trails: 2 in zone 1, 3 in zone 2 and 5 in zone 3. Stop beside one and start it, and 10 orbs light up on a loop of trail that leaves the terminal and comes back to it (each terminal has its own loop, a few hundred metres long). Collect all 10 and ski back: the terminal powers on by itself as you reach it, with no stop or prompt. Power every terminal in a zone, then ski up to the way onward's control to open it (see below). Only one terminal runs at a time. Each terminal has an antenna light: red while dormant, yellow (pulsing) while running and green once powered. Its screen and a row of ten lights, which fill as you collect its orbs, match.
- **Gate and bridge controls:** the east gate, the drawbridge and the south gate each have a control facing the side you arrive from: on the lintel of each gate, and on a post on the north bank for the drawbridge. It has one indicator light per terminal in its zone, red until that terminal is powered and then green, and an antenna light that stays red until you ski up to the control with every terminal powered. That opens the way, and the antenna turns green. Ski up to it before then and it tells you how many terminals are still to power.
- **The altar:** on the dais at the centre of the Broken Circle. Once every zone 3 terminal is powered, stop at it to wake it: the 20 purple crystals appear. Bring them all in, then come back to the altar to open the Crystal Gate.
- **Prompts:** coming to a stop beside a terminal or the altar opens a full-screen prompt that takes all input until you answer it. Keyboard: Space (or Enter) to go ahead, X (or Esc) to cancel. Controller: A to go ahead, X (or B) to cancel. The triggers don't cancel, since it's easy to squeeze one skiing up. Touch: tap the buttons. A prompt that's only telling you something (say, another terminal is still running) has a single button. Once dismissed, it won't open again until you've moved away and come back.
- **Counter and map:** the counter at the top shows the running terminal's orbs, or the crystals once the altar is awake, or otherwise how many of the zone's terminals are powered. The map shows the terminals (red dormant, yellow running, green powered), the running terminal's orbs, the crystals and the altar.
- **Saving:** terminals, orbs, the altar and crystals are saved in your browser, so gates and the bridge stay open between visits (saves from before the terminals keep any zone you'd finished). **Reset skier** in dev view clears that progress. For testing, the **Zone 1 / 2 / 3 terminals powered** and **Crystals collected** sliders in dev view's Tuning panel set them directly; maxing one opens that zone's gate or lowers its bridge, and maxing the crystals opens the Crystal Gate.


- **Zone 1 (north-west):** the original trail network, enclosed by an irregular range of impassable mountains. Power its two terminals to open the **East Gate** in the wide, flat pass on the east side.
- **Zone 2 (north-east):** past the gate. It's hillier, with five winding trails (green to black) that all lead to the river. Power its three terminals to lower the **drawbridge** into zone 3. Steel-plated ramps on slate abutments meet the snow flush on both banks, and the lowered deck skis like groomed trail. A spur ridge runs from zone 1's mountains to the river, so zone 2 can't reach the strip below zone 1 without crossing the river first.
- **Outpost:** zones 1 and 2 hold a sci-fi research outpost. Zone 1 has a habitat dome, a research tower and a landing pad. Zone 2 has a processing plant, a mine excavator, a greenhouse complex, a bio lab and a comms array. Smaller equipment (weather stations, fuel tanks, solar arrays, haul vehicles) sits just off the trails. Buildings are solid and show on the map. What you bump into is read off each model's actual shape at body height, so walls are where you see them, and you can ski under overhead parts such as the Spine Arch.
- **Gates and bridges:** the East and South Gates and both river bridges are outpost tech, like the buildings. The gates have white pylons with light strips, a barrier of panels out to the slopes, a steel lintel and armoured door leaves; the lintel carries the gate's control (indicator lights and an antenna, red until opened, then green). The bridges have steel decks with light rails, white bollards, and (on the drawbridge) white lifting pylons. The Crystal Gate keeps its own look.
- **Zone 3 (the southern half):** across the river, around a frozen lake you can ski across (the ice runs like groomed trail). Six trails circle and cross the lake, with five terminals. Instead of outpost buildings, it holds the remnants of an alien civilisation: the Sleeping Ribs (the bones of a full-grown purple moose about eleven times the size of the living ones, lying where it fell asleep: the hump of spines over its shoulders, an arched rib cage over a cluster of purple crystal where its heart was, six folded legs, and its skull and palmate antlers on the snow with the tentacle beard scattered as beads of bone), the Spine Arch (which Bridge Road passes under), the Watcher spire and the Broken Circle, plus bone shards. Makeshift camps sit among them: tents and tarps, stilt shacks, ice harvesters on the shore and signal towers. Powering all five terminals opens the **South Gate** and lets the altar wake.
- **Lawns:** every off-trail landmark sits on a broad groomed lawn with a wandering, non-circular outline. A curving groomed tongue joins it to the nearest trail; the tongue is about as wide as the lawn, swells and narrows along its length, and tapers toward the trail. Lawns and tongues that come close melt together into one shape. Trails and lawns are drawn as one groomed shape with no rim line: groomed snow meets the open snow along a soft edge, and every corner (bends, junctions and trail-to-lawn joins) is rounded off. They ski like trail, stop at water and rock, and aren't signed or shown on the map.
- **Purple crystals:** once the altar is woken, 20 crystals appear: 5 in zone 1, 5 in zone 2 and 10 in zone 3. Each sits beside an off-trail landmark (a building, ruin or camp), always in the open with nothing built over it, marked by a 10 m column of purple light you can see from far off. The counter switches to crystals, and the map shows them as purple diamonds.
- **Zone 4:** south of zone 3 beyond a mountain divide across the whole map, the same size as zone 3. Bringing all 20 crystals back to the altar swings open the 15 m **Crystal Gate** in the divide, south of the Broken Circle. Zone 4 is open snowfields with no buildings or people: winding Frost Road (blue), scattered outcrops of purple crystal, herds of purple moose, and a giant. The giant is a grown moose as big as the skeleton by the lake, asleep in the middle of Frost Road's loop on a broad groomed lawn, which the green **Giant's Way** crosses as it cuts across the loop, its chest rising and falling and a cloud of breath rolling from its muzzle every few seconds; it's solid. Five herds straddle Frost Road, so you ski straight through them, and another lies at the far southern end of the Herd Trail. In each, some graze and wander and some lie asleep in the snow, with antlerless calves and big bulls one and a half to two times the size of a grown moose.
- **Wildlife:** six-legged purple tentacle moose walk back and forth on their own paths beside the trails, one in zone 2 and ten in zone 3. They walk with an alternating tripod gait, and their tentacle beards sway as they go. They're solid, so ski round them. People and animals aren't shown on the map.
- **People:** outpost crews and settlers in parkas stand about by the buildings and camps, or walk slow loops around them. In zone 3, the short, blue-skinned natives keep to the ruins: they have long swept-back ears and brown robes whose trains drag behind them. They glide round the big ruins and stand near them and the bone shards. Everyone is solid, so ski round them.
- **The way back:** a fixed lower bridge crosses from zone 3 to the strip below zone 1. From there, black-rated **Switchbacks** climb benches cut into the mountainside to a saddle, where the South Gate leads down into zone 1's Home Stretch.

## Interface

- **Play view (default):**
  - Map and distance in the bottom left.
  - Grade, cadence and speed in the bottom right.
  - Stick diagrams with LT / RT readouts and the current mode in the centre. Trigger kicks show on the opposite stick, and when you're idle the diagrams demo the intended rhythm.
  - A controller-layout panel in the top right.
- **Dev view:** the full HUD, Tuning and stick debug tools. Toggle it with the **Dev** button or the **`** key.

## Mechanics

- **Skating:** your body rides the ski you're on, so your path weaves around the line between your skis. The skate V widens or narrows in fixed steps with speed and slope, so both sides stay even.
- **Camera:** it follows the line between your skis, so alternating strides cancel out and only real turns move the view.
- **Ski tracks:** marks are straight, the way a ski is, and are reset when you drift off a ski's line. Kick turns leave a herringbone pattern. Older marks fade into the snow as new ones are laid: the newest 50 are at the mark strength (70% by default, a slider), then about 64%, 51% and 31% for the next three groups of 10, and anything older than 80 marks back stays at 15%. Fading mixes each mark's colour toward the snow it lies on (groomed, open snow or lake ice) rather than making it see-through, so crossing marks never darken each other. Skis lie along the slope under them, tilted from tip to tail and side to side, and always sit on top of their marks.
- **Terrain and trails:**
  - Rolling terrain with a ridge and a valley, and gravity acts along the skis.
  - Groomed trails with green, blue and black ratings, and scattered trees: six in zone 1, the East Gate connector, and five in zone 2.
  - The open snow is a light, clear ice blue rather than white (only groomed snow is white), and the steep mountain faces are a paler icy blue. It's textured with wind ripples (sastrugi) that come and go in patches, under broad soft drifts. Their brightest crests are a pale blue, so only groomed snow is white. The two patterns repeat at different sizes, so the ground reads in perspective without a grid's uniform look. There are no contour lines. Trees grow only on the valley floors and the mountains' lower skirts, never on steep ground.
- **Palette:** everything shares one wintry palette, in shades and tints of a few core colours: slate blue and snow white for the outpost and the people's gear; blue-green spruce; grey-brown bark, timber and robes; muted rust and ochre accents (the skier's jacket, tarps and some parkas); glacier blue for the natives; and dusk violet for the moose and the crystals.
  - Mountains, the river and the map edge stop you. The ground loads in tiles as you ski, and a coarse mesh carries the distant skyline.
  - Ungroomed snow is slower, except in classic. Double poles keep their full push off the trail.
- **Trail assist:** gently steers your stride toward the trail ahead. It aims across the centre line at the mirror of your offset (the **centring** slider sets how far), so drifting to one edge pulls you back through the middle instead of letting you settle off to the side. Centring fades out near junctions and trail ends, and after switching trails the assist (and its centring) eases back in over the trail-switch time, so changing trails never yanks you sideways. It lets go when you lean, kick-turn or steer away. At junctions it works with kick turns instead:
  - **Snap to trails:** within 20 m of a junction, a same-side kick that would swing past one of the trails leaving it (or end just short of one) stops lined up with it instead. That includes the trail you came in on, so a full kick turn stops pointing back the way you came. A snapped kick doesn't use up a step of the build: the next kick carries the step the snapped one would have had (for example 15°, 25°, snap onto the trail, then 40°), so you can carry on round to the next trail.
  - **Settle:** after a kick turn at a junction, the assist comes back as soon as the kick-turn window closes and eases in quickly (0.3 s instead of 1.5 s), pulling you onto the trail you're nearly lined up with.
  - The snap distance is a slider in the Trail assist group (0 turns it off).
- **Skier:** V2 for stick strokes (load tall, push and compress, recover) and V1 offset poling for trigger and key kicks, with tuck, snow plow and classic poses.
- **Tuning:** every setting is a slider in the Tuning panel, grouped into Controller, Touch, Kicks and strides, Skate angle, Snow and glide, Classic/poling/braking, Trail assist, Camera and Testing. Only sliders you've moved are remembered in your browser; the rest follow the current defaults. Physics runs in fixed steps of at most 1/60 s, so it behaves the same at any frame rate. The stick debug recorder logs your stick input so you can tune the input thresholds.
